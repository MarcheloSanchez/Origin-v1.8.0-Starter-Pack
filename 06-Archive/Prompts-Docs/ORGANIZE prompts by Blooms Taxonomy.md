**Bloom's Taxonomy** is ==a hierarchical framework for categorizing educational learning objectives into levels of complexity==. It was developed by educational psychologist Benjamin Bloom and colleagues in the 1950s and has been revised over time. The taxonomy is used to structure curricula, assessments, and teaching methods to foster different types of learning, from basic recall to complex creation and evaluation.
1. Domains of Learning:
- **Cognitive Domain:** This domain focuses on intellectual capabilities, knowledge, and thinking processes.
- **Affective Domain:** This domain deals with emotions, attitudes, values, and appreciation.
- **Psychomotor Domain:** This domain focuses on physical skills and abilities. 
2. Levels of the Cognitive Domain (Original):
- **Knowledge:** Recalling facts and basic concepts.
- **Comprehension:** Explaining ideas or concepts.
- **Application:** Using information in a new situation.
- **Analysis:** Breaking down information into parts to explore relationships.
- **Synthesis:** Creating something new by combining different ideas.
- **Evaluation:** Judging the value of information or ideas based on criteria. 
3. Levels of the Cognitive Domain (Revised):
- **Remembering:** Recalling facts and basic concepts.
- **Understanding:** Explaining ideas or concepts.
- **Applying:** Using information in a new situation.
- **Analyzing:** Breaking down information into parts to explore relationships.
- **Evaluating:** Making judgments based on criteria.
- **Creating:** Producing new or original work. 
4. Importance and Use:
- Bloom's Taxonomy provides a framework for educators to develop learning outcomes, design assessments, and align teaching methods with specific learning goals. 
- It helps educators ensure that learning activities progress from basic recall to higher-order thinking skills. 
- It can be used to create more effective and engaging learning experiences for students.

## Prompt Templates and Walk-Throughs

## Remember → Understand Bridge

> **Template:**  
> “First, **list** the key terms related to [TOPIC]. Then **explain** each term in two sentences suitable for a ninth-grade audience.”

**Why it works:** Combines Level 1 recall with Level 2 comprehension, yielding both a glossary and clear explanations in one call.[](https://teaching.utoronto.ca/resources/active-verbs-for-blooms-revised-taxonomy/)[](https://teaching.utoronto.ca/resources/active-verbs-for-blooms-revised-taxonomy/)

## Apply in Technical Troubleshooting

> **Prompt:** “Apply the five-step DMAIC process to improve the throughput of a website experiencing 2-second latency spikes during peak traffic.”

**Expected Output:** A concrete DMAIC-structured action plan including Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control stages (Level 3).[](https://www.coloradocollege.edu/other/assessment/how-to-assess-learning/learning-outcomes/blooms-revised-taxonomy.html)[](https://www.coloradocollege.edu/other/assessment/how-to-assess-learning/learning-outcomes/blooms-revised-taxonomy.html)

## Analyze-Evaluate Combo for Policy Review

> **Prompt:** “Analyze the 2023 EU AI Act to identify three potential compliance gaps for startups, then evaluate which gap poses the highest legal risk and justify your ranking.”

Combines Level 4 analysis with Level 5 evaluation to drive nuanced, defensible conclusions.[](https://www.aiforeducation.io/prompts/blooms-taxonomy)[](https://www.aiforeducation.io/prompts/blooms-taxonomy)

## Create for Lesson Design

> **Prompt:**  
> “You are a veteran curriculum designer. **Design** a project-based learning unit on renewable energy for 10th-grade science lasting 4 weeks. Include objectives, activities, Bloom-aligned assessments, and a rubric.”

Leverages Level 6 for original instructional materials embedding Bloom verbs throughout the deliverables.

- **Assess**
- **Rank**
- **Interpret**
- **Infer**

- **Generate**
- **Create**
- **Draft**
- **Design**

- **Suggest**
- **Formulate**
